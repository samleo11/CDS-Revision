☁ CLOUDS

Beginner-friendly • exam-ready • visual recognition first

CDS strategy: Learn the name clue → appearance → height → weather. In most questions, one strong clue is enough to identify the cloud.

A. THE BIG PICTURE

What is a cloud?
Tiny water droplets and/or ice crystals suspended in the air. Water vapour itself is invisible; the tiny droplets/ice make a cloud visible.
Name clues
  • Cirro → high-level
  • Alto → middle-level
  • Strato → layered/spread out
  • Cumulo → heaped/puffy
  • Nimbo/Nimbus → rain-bearing
One exception to remember
Nimbostratus is usually placed in the middle level, but is vertically thick and can extend into other levels. Cumulus/Cumulonimbus usually have low bases but may grow upward greatly.

B. SIMPLE CLASSIFICATION

HIGH

Cirrus • Cirrocumulus • Cirrostratus

Usually high; predominantly ice crystals.
MIDDLE

Altocumulus • Altostratus • Nimbostratus

Usually middle-level; water droplets and/or ice.
LOW

Stratus • Stratocumulus • Cumulus

Usually low; mainly water droplets.
VERTICAL

Cumulus • Cumulonimbus

Low base is common, but strong upward growth.

Altitude note for CDS: WMO cloud “levels” overlap and vary with latitude. A useful standard guide is: high ≈ 6 km upward in the tropics; middle ≈ 2–8 km; low bases ≈ surface–2 km. Do not treat these as universal fixed boundaries. “Height category” refers mainly to where the cloud genus is normally encountered—not necessarily its entire vertical extent.

HEIGHT MAP — EXAM FOCUS

HIGH • Cirrus • Cirrocumulus • Cirrostratus ≈ 6–18 km in tropical regions
MIDDLE • Altocumulus • Altostratus • Nimbostratus ≈ 2–8 km in tropical regions
LOW • Stratus • Stratocumulus • Cumulus bases commonly below ≈ 2 km
VERTICAL DEVELOPMENT • Cumulus / especially Cumulonimbus: low base may grow through middle and high levels

C. CLOUD-BY-CLOUD REVISION

Cirrus (Ci)

HIGH

Looks: thin, wispy, feather/hair-like.
Weather: usually no precipitation reaching ground.
Composition: ice crystals.
CDS clue: “Thin and wispy high cloud” → Cirrus.
Memory: Cirrus = “hair in the sky”.

Cirrocumulus (Cc)

HIGH

Looks: very small white cloudlets in ripples/grains; often “fish-scale”.
Weather: no significant precipitation.
Composition: predominantly ice crystals.
CDS clue: Mackerel sky / tiny ripples.
Memory: Cirro + cumulo = high + tiny heaps.

Cirrostratus (Cs)

HIGH

Looks: thin, transparent, veil-like sheet.
Weather: no significant precipitation.
Composition: ice crystals.
CDS clue: may produce a halo around Sun/Moon.
Memory: Cirro + strato = high veil.

Altocumulus (Ac)

MIDDLE

Looks: rounded patches/rolls/cloudlets, generally larger than cirrocumulus and often shaded.
Weather: usually little/no significant precipitation reaching ground.
Composition: water droplets and/or ice.
CDS clue: middle-level puffy patches.

Altostratus (As)

MIDDLE

Looks: broad grey/blue-grey, fairly uniform layer.
Weather: may precede widespread precipitation; often no significant precipitation itself at a given observation.
Composition: mixed water droplets and ice crystals.
CDS clue: Sun may appear dimly through it.

Stratus (St)

LOW

Looks: low, uniform grey layer—like lifted fog.
Weather: overcast; may give light drizzle.
Composition: mainly water droplets.
CDS clue: low blanket/featureless layer.

Stratocumulus (Sc)

LOW

Looks: low, broad rounded rolls/lumps arranged in a layer.
Weather: generally little precipitation; sometimes light drizzle.
Composition: mainly water droplets.
CDS clue: low + layered + lumpy.

Cumulus (Cu)

LOW BASE / VERTICAL

Looks: white cotton-wool/cauliflower heap, usually with a flat base.
Weather: commonly fair weather when small; can grow vertically.
Composition: mainly water droplets; upper parts of larger clouds may contain ice.
CDS clue: puffy + flat base.
Memory: Cumulo = heap.

Nimbostratus (Ns)

USUALLY MIDDLE / THICK

Looks: dark, thick, widespread, featureless layer.
Weather: continuous, widespread, persistent rain/snow; generally not thunderstorm weather.
Composition: water droplets + ice crystals (varies vertically).
CDS clue: steady widespread rain + layer.
Memory: Nimbo = rain; stratus = layer.

Cumulonimbus (Cb)

STRONG VERTICAL DEVELOPMENT

Looks: huge towering cauliflower cloud; often a fibrous anvil-shaped top.
Weather: heavy showers, thunder, lightning, hail.
Composition: water droplets + ice crystals, especially aloft.
CDS clue: thunderstorm / hail / anvil → Cumulonimbus.
Memory: Cumulo = tower + nimbus = rain.

⚠️ DO NOT CONFUSE

Cumulus vs Cumulonimbus
Cu: puffy, flat base; small/fair-weather form.
Cb: massive tower, often anvil; thunder/lightning/hail.
Strongest clue: Thunderstorm = Cb.
Cirrocumulus vs Altocumulus
Cc: high, very small white ripples/cloudlets, predominantly ice.
Ac: middle, larger cloudlets/patches, often shaded.
Strongest clue: Tiny high “fish scales” = Cc.
Nimbostratus vs Cumulonimbus
Ns: dark, widespread layer; steady continuous rain.
Cb: towering convective cloud; heavy showers/thunder/hail.
Strongest clue: Thunder/lightning/hail = Cb, not Ns.
Cirrus vs Cirrocumulus
Ci: wispy filaments/feathers.
Cc: tiny rounded ripples/cloudlets.
Strongest clue: Wisps = Ci; scales = Cc.
Stratus vs Nimbostratus
St: low uniform grey layer; possible light drizzle.
Ns: much thicker/darker widespread layer; persistent rain.
Strongest clue: Continuous widespread rain = Ns.

CDS HEIGHT ORDER — QUICK REVISION

Safest exam order:
High: Cirrus / Cirrocumulus / Cirrostratus → Middle: Altocumulus / Altostratus / Nimbostratus → Low: Stratus / Stratocumulus / Cumulus → Vertical: Cumulus and especially Cumulonimbus can rise upward through several levels.
Do not force every cloud into one rigid “base-to-top” ranking: clouds overlap vertically and bases vary. In CDS, category recognition is more reliable.

📝 PRACTICE — CDS-STYLE MCQs

1. A cloud is thin, white and feather-like, occurring at a high level. It is most likely: (a) Stratus (b) Cirrus (c) Altostratus (d) Cumulus
2. “Mackerel sky” is most closely associated with: (a) Cirrocumulus (b) Cumulonimbus (c) Nimbostratus (d) Stratus
3. Which cloud is most characteristically associated with thunder, lightning and hail? (a) Nimbostratus (b) Cumulus (c) Cumulonimbus (d) Altostratus
4. Consider the following name clues: 1. Cirro—high level 2. Alto—middle level 3. Strato—layered. Which are correctly matched? (a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
5. A dark, thick, widespread, featureless cloud producing persistent rain is most likely: (a) Nimbostratus (b) Cumulus (c) Cirrocumulus (d) Altocumulus
6. Which one of the following is a high cloud? (a) Altostratus (b) Cirrostratus (c) Stratocumulus (d) Nimbostratus
7. Which feature best distinguishes cumulonimbus from nimbostratus? (a) Both can produce rain (b) Cumulonimbus is associated with thunder/lightning (c) Nimbostratus is layered (d) Both may be extensive
8. A puffy, cotton-wool cloud with a relatively flat base is: (a) Cumulus (b) Cirrus (c) Stratus (d) Altostratus
9. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched? (a) Cirrocumulus—large low-level rolls (b) Altocumulus—middle-level rounded patches (c) Stratus—towering anvil (d) Nimbostratus—thunderstorm cloud
10. Which sequence contains only the three standard high-level cloud genera? (a) Cirrus, Cirrocumulus, Cirrostratus (b) Cumulus, Stratus, Stratocumulus (c) Altocumulus, Altostratus, Nimbostratus (d) Cirrus, Altocumulus, Stratus
11. Consider the following statements about nimbostratus: 1. It is characteristically a widespread layered cloud. 2. It is associated with persistent precipitation. 3. Thunder and lightning are its defining features. Which are correct? (a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
12. The cloud most likely to show a transparent veil and sometimes a halo around the Sun or Moon is: (a) Cirrostratus (b) Stratus (c) Cumulus (d) Stratocumulus
13. Which statement is most accurate? (a) All cumulus clouds remain entirely low-level. (b) Cumulonimbus has a low base but can extend vertically into higher levels. (c) Nimbostratus is a high cloud only. (d) Cloud levels have identical altitude limits at all latitudes.
14. Which one is the best match? (a) Stratus—low, uniform layer (b) Cirrus—middle, dark layer (c) Altostratus—high, hair-like cloud (d) Cumulonimbus—thin transparent veil
15. A question describes “small, regularly arranged white cloudlets producing a rippled appearance high in the sky.” The answer is: (a) Altocumulus (b) Cirrocumulus (c) Stratocumulus (d) Cumulus

30-SECOND FINAL REVISION

Cirro = high • Alto = middle • Strato = layer • Cumulo = heap • Nimbo = rain.
Wispy → Cirrus | Fish scales → Cirrocumulus | Halo veil → Cirrostratus | Puffy flat base → Cumulus | Persistent rain layer → Nimbostratus | Anvil + thunder/hail → Cumulonimbus.

Accuracy basis: World Meteorological Organization (International Cloud Atlas) for the ten cloud genera, cloud levels and the distinction between cloud-base height and vertical extent; UK Met Office for standard visual and weather-recognition descriptions. Altitude bands are deliberately qualified because WMO states level limits overlap and vary with latitude.
