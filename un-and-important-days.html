04 · CDS PYQ-Inspired Practice

Built from the recurring 2021–2025 CDS formats: direct-date questions, match-the-pair, statement-based identification and organisation-linkage.

Q1. 24 January is observed as which of the following?
Answer: International Day of Education. The United Nations General Assembly proclaimed 24 January as the International Day of Education.
Q2. Consider the following pairs: 1. 22 March — World Water Day; 2. 5 June — World Environment Day; 3. 17 June — World Day to Combat Desertification and Drought. How many are correctly matched?
Answer: All three. This is a CDS-style match/association recall pattern.
Q3. World Press Freedom Day is observed on 3 May. Which organisation has the strongest institutional association with this observance?
Answer: United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO). The observance follows UNESCO’s recommendation and is closely associated with the 1991 Windhoek Declaration.
Q4. The International Day of Yoga is observed on 21 June. Which country played the leading role in proposing the observance at the United Nations?
Answer: India. The United Nations General Assembly adopted the relevant resolution in 2014 following India’s initiative.
Q5. 30 June is observed as the International Day of Parliamentarism because it is associated with the founding of which organisation?
Answer: Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU), founded on 30 June 1889.
Q6. Which one of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched? (a) 15 July — World Youth Skills Day (b) 12 August — International Youth Day (c) 29 August — International Day against Nuclear Tests (d) 8 September — International Day of Democracy
Answer: (d). 8 September is International Literacy Day; International Day of Democracy is 15 September.
Q7. The International Day against Nuclear Tests is observed on 29 August. Which broad theme is most directly associated with it?
Answer: Nuclear disarmament, non-proliferation and the cessation of nuclear testing.
Q8. Which day is observed on 15 September? (a) International Day of Peace (b) International Day of Democracy (c) International Literacy Day (d) International Anti-Corruption Day
Answer: (b) International Day of Democracy.
Q9. Which of the following statements about women freedom fighters is correct? (a) Usha Mehta is associated with Congress Radio (b) Captain Lakshmi Sahgal led the Rani of Jhansi Regiment (c) Sucheta Kriplani became India’s first woman Chief Minister (d) All of the above
Answer: (d) All of the above.
Q10. “Since wars begin in the minds of men…” is a famous phrase from the Constitution of which UN specialised agency?
Answer: United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO).
Q11. “Atoms for Peace” is most closely associated with which organisation?
Answer: International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA).
Q12. Which UN body has the primary responsibility for the maintenance of international peace and security under Article 24 of the UN Charter?
Answer: United Nations Security Council (UNSC).

⭐ What the CDS PYQs suggest

Across the 2021–2025 papers, the exam repeatedly rewards date ↔ observance, observance ↔ organisation, match-the-pair, and distinctive institutional phrase ↔ organisation recall. CDS-I 2025 directly asked the date of the International Day of Democracy; CDS-II 2023 tested International Day of Yoga themes; and CDS papers also use organisation-identification questions built around distinctive constitutional or founding phrases.

02B · Women Bharat Ratna Recipients

Complete high-yield set: 5 women recipients through 2024.

1971
Indira Gandhi
Public Affairs • former Prime Minister

First woman Prime Minister of India. Bharat Ratna in 1971. Led India during the 1971 war and Bangladesh’s liberation.

⚠️ Do not confuse “first woman PM” with “first woman President” — India’s first woman President was Pratibha Patil, who was not a Bharat Ratna recipient.
1980
Mother Teresa
Social Work / humanitarian service

Bharat Ratna in 1980. Founded Missionaries of Charity in 1950; Nobel Peace Prize in 1979.

⭐ Bharat Ratna ≠ Nobel Prize: she received both, in consecutive years.
1997
Aruna Asaf Ali
Public Affairs • freedom movement

Prominent Quit India leader; famously hoisted the Congress flag at Gowalia Tank, Bombay on 9 Aug 1942 and went underground.

⚠️ 1997 = Aruna Asaf Ali; do not assign her 1942 role to Sarojini Naidu.
1998
M. S. Subbulakshmi
Art / Carnatic music

Bharat Ratna in 1998. One of India’s most celebrated Carnatic vocalists; associated with international performances and devotional music.

⭐ Key distinction: first musician to receive Bharat Ratna.
2001
Lata Mangeshkar
Art / playback singing

Bharat Ratna in 2001. Legendary Indian playback singer with an exceptional contribution to Indian cinema and music.

⭐ Gazette category: Art – Music. Do not confuse with Padma awards.

⭐ Award sanity check

Women Bharat Ratna sequence: Indira Gandhi (1971) → Mother Teresa (1980) → Aruna Asaf Ali (1997) → M. S. Subbulakshmi (1998) → Lata Mangeshkar (2001).
No later woman recipient has been added through the 2024 Bharat Ratna awards. The official Government of India awards portal is the safest final check for award-year MCQs.

03 · UN / UN-System “Identification Phrases”

Use these as reverse-MCQ clues: phrase → organisation.

UNESCO

“Since wars begin in the minds of men…” → defences of peace must be constructed in the minds.
Deals with: education, science, culture, communication/information. Clue: intellectual & moral solidarity of humanity.

WHO

“The enjoyment of the highest attainable standard of health…”
Deals with: global public health. Clue: health is a fundamental human right; Constitution came into force 7 Apr 1948.

ILO

“Universal and lasting peace can be established only if it is based upon social justice.”
Deals with: labour standards, decent work, workers’ rights. Clue: unique tripartite structure — governments + employers + workers.

FAO

“Raising levels of nutrition and standards of living…” + freedom from hunger.
Deals with: food, agriculture, nutrition, rural populations. Clue: FAO = food/agriculture; not global monetary policy.

IAEA

“Atoms for Peace and Development.”
Deals with: peaceful nuclear science/technology, safeguards, safety and security. Clue: nuclear technology for peaceful purposes + non-proliferation/safeguards.

IMF

“International monetary cooperation through a permanent institution…”
Deals with: monetary system, exchange stability, balance-of-payments support, surveillance. Clue: Bretton Woods Articles of Agreement (1944).

World Bank / IBRD

“Reconstruction and development” by facilitating productive capital investment.
Deals with: development finance and policy support. Clue: IBRD ≠ IMF; Bank is development/investment focused.

UNDP

Core development focus: eradicate poverty, reduce inequality, build resilience and advance sustainable development.
Deals with: development policy/capacity, poverty, governance, resilience, SDGs. Clue: development programme — not refugee protection.

UNICEF

“For every child, every right.”
Deals with: child rights, survival, health, education and protection. Clue: mandate is rooted in the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child.

UNHCR

International protection + permanent solutions for refugees; also statelessness mandate.
Deals with: refugees, asylum-seekers, returnees, stateless persons and selected IDP situations. Clue: refugee/statelessness agency, not general migration agency.

UNFPA

“Every pregnancy is wanted, every childbirth is safe, and every young person’s potential is fulfilled.”
Deals with: sexual & reproductive health and rights, population issues, family planning. Clue: population/sexual-reproductive health, not children’s rights (UNICEF).

UNEP

Leading global authority on the environment; addresses the “triple planetary crisis”.
Deals with: climate change, nature/biodiversity loss, pollution/waste. Clue: UNEP hosts the UN Environment Assembly (UNEA).

WTO

Global system of trade rules; trade as a means to improve living standards, jobs and sustainable development.
Deals with: goods, services, IP, trade negotiations and disputes. Clue: WTO = trade rules; IMF = monetary system.

UN Security Council

“Primary responsibility for the maintenance of international peace and security.” — UN Charter Art. 24
Deals with: international peace/security. Clue: primary responsibility lies with UNSC, not UNGA.

UN General Assembly

Principal deliberative, policymaking and representative organ of the UN.
Deals with: broad deliberation, recommendations, elections/appointments under Charter, budget and international cooperation. Clue: one state = one vote in the GA.

ECOSOC

Central body for coordinating the UN’s economic, social and related work.
Deals with: economic/social/development coordination and policy review. Clue: ECOSOC = coordination in economic/social sphere.
