01 · CDS PATTERN — WHAT THE PAPERS ACTUALLY SHOW

What the papers show: CDS GK is not a newspaper-only paper. Across 2021–25, the actual papers repeatedly combine static GK with current-event framing. Examples include current awards and Yoga themes, recent schemes, defence/space developments, international events, and questions that convert a contemporary issue into a stable fact. The 2025-II paper opens with a chronology question on the Simla Agreement, Agra Summit, SAARC Charter and India’s first nuclear test; 2025 also contained current-affairs and defence/security questions alongside large static sections. Statement codes, matching, chronology and “which of the following” formats are routine.

Setter’s rule: the strongest current-affairs topics are not merely “big in the news”. They are events from which UPSC can extract a stable, one-line fact. In the recent papers, the recurring constructions are statement codes, matching, chronology, direct “which of the following” questions, country–organisation matching, scheme–objective matching and science/current event → basic concept. This is why the guide below deliberately favours static-linkable events over generic headline lists.

08 · EXAM-DAY FILTER

Do not over-revise volatile news. For wars, tariffs, negotiations and oil prices, memorise the durable facts: date → actors → geography → institution → strategic concept → India angle. For schemes/laws: name → ministry → objective → beneficiary → effective date. For summits: host → date → members → theme → institution. For defence: system → country → service → role.
Most important distinction: a topic being prominent in newspapers is not enough. A CDS-worthy topic is one where the setter can create a fair, four-option question that tests broad awareness without requiring an editorial opinion.
