5. Swadeshi & Anti-Partition Movement

Partition of Bengal — 1905→Anti-Partition protest→Swadeshi + Boycott→National Education→Broader mass politics
  • Partition: announced by Lord Curzon; came into effect 16 October 1905.
  • Swadeshi: use of indigenous goods; boycott of foreign goods; national education; public mobilisation.
  • Benares Congress 1905: Swadeshi resolution under Gokhale.
  • Calcutta Congress 1906: Dadabhai Naoroji; fourfold programme remembered as Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education.
  • Aurobindo Ghosh: favoured carrying Swadeshi beyond economic boycott toward a wider political mass struggle.
  • Annulment: Bengal partition annulled in 1911.
PYQ-linked — CDS I 2026: both statements about the anti-partition genesis and Aurobindo’s broader political vision were treated as correct.

PYQ-linked chronology: CDS I 2022 placed Ahmedabad Mill Strike before Foundation of Satyagraha Sabha and Jallianwala Bagh, with Champaran first.

Non-Cooperation Movement, 1920–22

  • Congress ratified NCM at Calcutta special session (Sept 1920) and adopted it at Nagpur (Dec 1920).
  • Programme: surrender titles, boycott schools/colleges/courts, boycott foreign cloth, constructive work, spinning/charkha, national institutions.
  • Not a programme of seizing control of state law-and-order machinery.
  • Chauri Chaura, 5 Feb 1922: police station burned; Gandhi withdrew the movement.
  • Swaraj Party, 1923: C.R. Das + Motilal Nehru; “pro-changers” entered legislatures to obstruct from within.
PYQ-linked: CDS II 2024 asked that wide distribution of charkhas was decided by the AICC in 1921.

Individual Satyagraha, 1940–41

  • First satyagrahi: Vinoba Bhave; second: Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • Issue: right to free expression against the war effort, without launching a mass movement.

10. Quit India Movement — 1942

Cripps Mission fails→Wardha CWC resolution, July 1942→AICC Bombay, 8 Aug→“Do or Die”→Mass uprising after arrests
  • Resolution adopted at Bombay on 8 August 1942; mass arrests followed on 9 August.
  • Gandhi’s call: “Do or Die”.
  • Congress leadership arrested almost immediately; movement became decentralised and often militant.
  • Underground activity: Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali and others.
  • Parallel governments: Ballia, Tamluk, Satara.
  • Notable sacrifices: Matangini Hazra (Bengal), Lakshman Naik (Odisha) — both linked to Quit India.
PYQ-linked: CDS II 2025 asked which movement saw Matangini Hazra and Lakshman Naik sacrifice their lives; answer: Quit India Movement.

Why INA matters: Military impact was limited, but the INA trials and post-war unrest affected Indian public opinion and confidence in the continued reliability of colonial armed forces. The 1946 Naval Mutiny was part of the wider atmosphere of post-war discontent.

12. Constitutional developments — freedom-struggle lens

MeasureKey CDS facts
Indian Councils Act 1861Legislative councils enlarged; portfolio system recognised; beginning of limited Indian association in legislation.
Indian Councils Act 1892Further enlargement; indirect election principle introduced in practice though “election” was not used formally.
Morley-Minto / Indian Councils Act 1909Separate electorates for Muslims; legislative councils enlarged.
Government of India Act 1919Dyarchy in provinces; bicameral central legislature; expanded franchise; separate electorates retained/extended.
Government of India Act 1935Provincial autonomy; proposed federation; Federal Court; expanded franchise; dyarchy proposed at centre; governors retained special powers.
Indian Independence Act 1947Created two dominions; ended British sovereignty; princely states no longer under British paramountcy; Constituent Assemblies became sovereign legislatures.

Commissions / reports

ItemRemember
Simon Commission, 1927All-British commission; chaired by Sir John Simon; boycott in India.
Nehru Report, 1928Motilal Nehru chair; proposed dominion status and constitutional framework.
Communal Award, 1932Ramsay MacDonald; separate electorates for several groups, including Depressed Classes.
Poona Pact, 1932Gandhi–Ambedkar agreement; reserved seats for Depressed Classes in joint electorates.
Cripps Mission, 1942Post-war dominion-status offer; provinces could opt out of proposed union; rejected by Congress and League for different reasons.
Cabinet Mission, 1946Three-level concept: Union + groups of provinces + provinces; weak centre limited mainly to defence, foreign affairs and communications; Constituent Assembly proposed.
PYQ-linked: CDS II 2024 asked Cabinet Mission statements and the Second RTC/CDM sequence. The official constitutional archive provides the original 1919 and 1935 texts.

20. CDS-style practice — 10 MCQs

Q1 — Easy

Who was the first president of the Indian National Congress?

A. Dadabhai Naoroji
B. W.C. Bonnerjee
C. A.O. Hume
D. Surendranath Banerjea

Answer: B

A.O. Hume was a key founder/organiser, but W.C. Bonnerjee presided over the first session.

Q2 — Easy

Gandhi’s first major satyagraha in India was associated with:

A. Kheda
B. Champaran
C. Bardoli
D. Ahmedabad

Answer: B

Champaran was 1917; Ahmedabad and Kheda followed in 1918.

Q3 — Easy

The “Do or Die” call is associated with:

A. Non-Cooperation Movement
B. Civil Disobedience Movement
C. Quit India Movement
D. Home Rule Movement

Answer: C

Gandhi used the phrase in the Quit India context in August 1942.

Q4 — Medium | Chronology

Arrange the following in chronological order:
1. Lucknow Pact 2. Champaran Satyagraha 3. Ahmedabad Mill Strike 4. Jallianwala Bagh

A. 1-2-3-4
B. 2-1-3-4
C. 1-3-2-4
D. 2-3-1-4

Answer: A

1916 → 1917 → 1918 → 1919.

Q5 — Medium | Statements

Consider the following statements about the Home Rule Movement:
1. Tilak and Annie Besant led separate Home Rule organisations in 1916.
2. The movement demanded self-government/Home Rule.
3. It was launched after the Quit India Movement.

A. 1 only   B. 1 and 2 only   C. 2 and 3 only   D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: B

Statement 3 is false: Home Rule preceded Quit India by more than two decades.

Q7 — Medium | Not correctly matched

Which one of the following is not correctly matched?

A. Annie Besant — First woman president of INC
B. Sarojini Naidu — First Indian woman president of INC

Answer: none of the above; all four are correctly matched.

This is deliberately a trap-style question: an examiner could instead insert “Sarojini Naidu — first woman president” to make it false.

Q8 — Hard | Statements

With reference to the Civil Disobedience Movement, consider:
1. The Dandi March was directed against the salt laws.
2. Gandhi attended the Second Round Table Conference after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact.
3. Gandhi attended all three Round Table Conferences.

A. 1 only   B. 1 and 2 only   C. 2 and 3 only   D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: B

He attended only the Second RTC.

Q9 — Hard | Organisation

Which of the following sequences best represents the organisational evolution of revolutionary nationalism?

A. HSRA → HRA → Abhinav Bharat
B. HRA → HSRA → INA
C. INA → HRA → HSRA
D. Ghadar → INA → HRA

Answer: B

HRA 1924 → HSRA 1928; INA developed in the 1940s. The other options reverse the broad chronology.

Q10 — Hard | Multi-statement

Consider the following statements:
1. The Government of India Act, 1919 introduced dyarchy in the provinces.
2. The Government of India Act, 1935 abolished dyarchy at the provincial level and provided for provincial autonomy.
3. The 1935 Act established the proposed Federation of India in full operation immediately.

A. 1 only   B. 1 and 2 only   C. 2 and 3 only   D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: B

The federal part of the 1935 Act was never brought fully into operation; the provincial provisions came into effect in 1937.

Answer Key: 1-B, 2-B, 3-C, 4-A, 5-B, 6-A, 7-None (trap), 8-B, 9-B, 10-B.
